## North Carolina: strongest direct match

[G.S. 84-2.2](https://www3.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_84/GS_84-2.2.html) expressly addresses websites generating documents from consumer answers. Its conditional exemption includes preview before purchase, NC-attorney review of every possible template component, attorney records available on request, a no-substitute-for-attorney message, provider legal name/physical address, consumer-remedy protections, no compelled out-of-state venue, and a conspicuous satisfaction/UPL-referral process. Registration and annual renewal are also required. The official text includes the 2025 amendment.

DocketStamp closely resembles the described workflow. Its dropdown variants and inserted legal material should be included in any template review. Renaming the fee as mailing does not establish an exemption. The [State Bar's provider page](https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/website-document-providers/) confirms the registration process; registration alone does not satisfy the other requirements.

**Evidence needed to release hold:** registration/renewal; NC reviewer and approved template versions/variants; compliant disclosures, terms, physical address and complaint process. The repository's Terms identify Western Amalgamated but the inspected page does not show a physical provider address or the conspicuous NC process. External compliance records were not available, so they are unknown, not proven absent.

## Arizona: preparer certification and software classification

Current official [ACJA 7-208, October 2025](https://www.azcourts.gov/Portals/0/0/admcode/pdfcurrentcode/7-208%20Legal%20Document%20Preparer%2010-2025.pdf), §§A/B/E/F/J, regulates individuals and entities preparing legal documents for unrepresented people; merely secretarial/receptionist work is distinguished. Certification or Arizona-attorney supervision, business/principal requirements, document identification and nonlawyer disclosures are relevant. No express software exemption was located in this provision. That absence does not itself settle a SaaS classification.

The judiciary's [program page](https://www.azcourts.gov/cld/Legal-Document-Preparer-Program/LegalDocumentPrepaperBoardInformation) and [FAQ](https://www.azcourts.gov/cld/Legal-Document-Preparer-Program/FAQ) make clear that the framework is not confined to pleadings. Mailing rather than filing is therefore an insufficient exemption theory.

**Evidence needed:** written analysis of the fully consumer-operated software and fulfillment model, or an appropriate certified/supervised structure with required operational controls. The [September 2026 proposal](https://acjaforum.azcourts.gov/ACJA-Forum/aft/1964) references November 2026 changes; a proposal is not an adopted rule. Recheck operative orders before launch after November 15, 2026.

## California: ministerial assistance is not automatically exempt

BPC §§6400–6402 distinguish compensated self-help document assistance from merely secretarial activity and certain published information. The [State Bar's official statutory compilation](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/Publication-250-Volume-2.pdf), printed pp.83–85, describes the framework. It is an older compilation: direct legislative-site retrieval returned 403, so this review does not claim it verifies every October 2026 provision. The judiciary's [legislative summary](https://courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/courts/default/2024-10/2023_summaryofcourt-relatedlegislation_121523.pdf) records extension of the framework to January 1, 2030.

If the LDA framework applies, qualifications, registration, bonding, advertising/disclosures and contract requirements need review. [Current Placer County instructions](https://www.placer.ca.gov/10966/Legal-Document-Assistant) describe principal-business/branch-office registration and individual/corporate bond requirements. The [DCA explanation](https://www.dca.ca.gov/regulations/ccr3950_statement.pdf) corrects older every-county wording. Do not repeat that stale rule.

**Evidence needed:** current statute verification and counsel's assessment of software classification, California consumers/matters and provider physical operations; compliant LDA path if applicable. Neither incorporation elsewhere nor a California corporate recipient alone resolves territorial applicability.

## Nevada: an express software exception

[NRS 240A.030(3)(k)](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nRs/NRS-240A.html) excludes providers of legal forms or computer programs enabling another person to create legal documents. The official chapter reflects 2025 legislation, revised April 2026. This is a meaningful distinction from Arizona.

If additional services fall outside the exclusion, registration/bonding (§§.100–.126), disclosures/contracts (§§.150/.180/.190) and service limits (§.240) require review. The statute does not expressly decide DocketStamp's automated, user-approved print-and-mail bundle. Section.310 also preserves other UPL limits.

**Evidence needed:** opinion on software plus fulfillment, staff involvement, document selection and communications. Do not assume registration authorizes negotiation or representation. A precautionary temporary hold is reasonable if a decision must be made before this review, but Nevada should not be described as categorically banning software-generated letters.

## Texas and Washington: conditional paths

[Texas Government Code 81.101(c)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=81.101&chapter=GV.81&code=GV&tab=1) excludes forms/software and similar products when they conspicuously state that they do not substitute for attorney advice. It preserves Chapter 83 and does not settle additional personalized services. The current general “not a law firm/no advice” disclosure should not be assumed equivalent to the exact required message. Preserve the locked footer; assess any additional placement/message separately. The official indexed section was accessible; some legacy chapter URLs redirected.

[Washington GR 24](https://www.courts.wa.gov/court_rules/pdf/GR/GA_GR_24_00_00.pdf), amended September 2025, permits sale of legal forms in any format and general legal information. Its general definition also includes document selection/completion and applying legal judgment to another person's circumstances. Determine whether actual guided logic and assistance remain within the exception; a disclaimer alone does not resolve that question.

## Florida: unresolved, not cleared

The [Florida Bar's ethics guidance](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etarticles/ethics-alert-business-arrangements-with-nonlawyers/) distinguishes blank-form sales/transcription from advice and customized services in its discussed contexts. It should not be extrapolated into blanket authorization for all automated demand letters. [Chapter 10 of the Bar rules](https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/06/2026_12-JUNE-Chapter-10-RRTFB.pdf) was indexed but direct retrieval returned 403. Obtain and review operative Rule 10-2.2, relevant decisions and the software model before inclusion. No categorical Florida legal conclusion is made here.

## Separate credit-dispute product issue

[15 USC 1679a(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section1679a) defines a credit repair organization broadly around paid services with an express or implied credit-improvement purpose, including related advice/assistance. Its stated exclusions do not create a general software/postage exemption. Supplying paid FCRA dispute categories, populated letters and mailing creates a credible classification question even without score-improvement promises.

If covered, [15 USC 1679b–e](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap41-subchapII-A.htm) govern advance payment, separate disclosure, written contract, cancellation and a three-business-day service waiting period. The [FTC summary](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/credit-repair-organizations-act) confirms the payment/contract restrictions. Freezing HTML before payment does not establish completion of a promised physical-mail service.

Texas has a separate [credit-services organization framework and registration guidance](https://www.sos.texas.gov/statdoc/cso.shtml), Finance Code Chapter 393. California's [DOJ guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/consumers/credit-services-organizations) describes its own registration/bond/contract rules under Civil Code §§1789.10 et seq. A state allowance does not override federal requirements.

**Recommendation:** focused national credit-cartridge classification review before launch under the current checkout; review marketing, SEO, affiliate/agent claims, consumer control, staff involvement, promised deliverables, fee allocation and completion/payment timing. This is an unresolved potential classification, not a finding that the business already is a CRO. Do not infer that all other letters are affected or that changing titles cures it.

## Geographic and enforcement design for a subsequent implementation

1. Gather consumer residence separately from sender mailing address. Gather structured property state for tenant instruments and any other legally relevant matter nexus. Recipient state is a separate fact; blocking recipients in California would unnecessarily exclude corporate addressees without establishing consumer jurisdiction.
2. Obtain a reviewed state × notice × service decision matrix, with sources, reviewer, approved template version, effective/review dates and reason. Distinguish unsupported, eligible under documented conditions, and unknown. Do not infer approved support from registry presence.
3. Enforce the same decision at the earliest relevant preparation entry and server-side before freezing/payment. Cover homepage/direct workspace, URL-prefilled drafts, REST stage/retrieval, MCP/WebMCP and direct checkout. Missing required residence/matter data should prevent progression through the relevant gate; do not infer it from IP alone.
4. Record policy and template versions, evidence references and required disclosure acceptance with the order. Preserve frozen paid content. Define an operator review/refund/fulfillment policy for affected existing paid orders; do not silently rewrite them.
5. Test direct API bypasses, stale staged links, unknown states, mixed consumer/property/recipient states, different products, exempt paths and preexisting paid snapshots. Show unsupported notice before taking payment.

**Implementation has not occurred.** This sprint's output is the concrete review and backlog needed for that launch decision. Existing product availability remains unchanged.
