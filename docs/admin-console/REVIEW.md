# Business-specific design review

## Business and authority

DocketStamp sells deterministic administrative notice preparation and physical USPS mailing, with guest checkout and optional account history. Its customers include tenants, consumers disputing credit/debt information, service/policy customers, and owners of connected hardware exercising supported consumer rights. The nine cartridges cover vacate, deposit, repair, credit dispute, debt validation, cancellation, pre-arbitration dispute, arbitration opt-out, and data deletion. It is a self-help utility, not a law firm; a mailing is not proof that a deposit was recovered, debt removed, contract canceled, or legal right successfully exercised.

Canonical project decisions are the [master](../DOCKETSTAMP_MASTER_BUILD_SPEC.md) and [agentic expansion](../DOCKETSTAMP_AGENTIC_EXPANSION.md) specifications supplied by project instructions: no fitness-membership business, hardware OEMs allowed, fixed templates and disclosures, one-page geometry, shared interpolation, freeze before payment, and isolated Lob verification/dispatch keys. These are requirements, not proof every path already complies.

Verified in local source: per-notice prices of $12.95, $19.95, and $27.95 USD, three mailing tiers, nine cartridges, notice workspace, lock endpoint, Stripe and Lob integrations. The price registry is `src/lib/docket/notices/index.ts:106`. The master specification's supplier and fee figures are planning estimates; no invoice or actual fee reconciliation was reviewed. No subscriptions are advertised on the active homepage.

Acquisition surfaces present in code: homepage chooser, company directory, SEO notice pages, recipient prefills, direct links, REST/MCP staged drafts, WebMCP, Google tags, Meta pixel and CAPI, and UTMs/click identifiers. Presence of integration code does not establish current paid spend, traffic, consent configuration, ad destinations, or successful delivery to ad platforms. The inherited Send My Notes brief describes a founder test and historical card traffic; it must not become a DocketStamp baseline.

Current intended journey: discover → select a compatible letter/recipient → complete details → review/adopt signature → choose delivery tier and email → freeze document/create intent → pay → verify payment → submit frozen document to Lob → mailing/tracking/proof. Agent staging is an acquisition/handoff mechanism; agents prepare, humans review/sign/pay. Fulfillment and proof are the purchased service. Actual recipient response is an additional, currently unverified outcome.

Operating needs follow directly: resolve paid-but-undispatched notices, ambiguous supplier submissions, address errors, stale locks, returned mail, missing proof/return receipt, payment mismatches, refunds/disputes, failed receipts, staging failures, and measurement gaps. Templates and recipient data require version/provenance control; the CEO must not gain an unrestricted legal-prose editor.

Assumptions proposed for implementation: a small operator team; reuse Next.js, Firebase/Firestore, Stripe, Lob, existing email and flag infrastructure; UTC storage with America/Chicago default reporting; USD checkout; no new paid vendor. None is a measured business baseline. SLA thresholds, retention, permissions and costs remain decisions listed in SPECIFICATION.md.

## Active implementation, not historical intent

`app/page.tsx` mounts the current homepage workbench; `app/notices/[slug]/page.tsx` mounts `src/components/docket/DocumentWorkspace.tsx` through the root-first `@/*` alias fallback in tsconfig. That workspace mounts `CheckoutModal`, which calls `/api/docket/lock-and-intent`. `lib/fulfillment/lob.ts` re-exports the adapter in `src/lib/fulfillment/lob.ts`.

`app/admin/page.tsx` is the active 3,471-line client page. Its six tabs are orders, incidents, margins, discounts, scenarios, and flags. The orders branch actually mounts CampaignPerformanceWidget, HighIntentCartsWidget, VisitorTelemetryDashboard, CohortAnalytics, and OrderJourneyDrawer. These are active, not merely leftover files. It calls `/api/admin/metrics`, which calls `getAdminDashboardMetrics()` in `lib/metrics-rollup.ts`.

The newer `computeFunnelConversionReport()` is exported and exercised in tests but has no active admin route/component caller in the inspected tree. The S6 evidence describes an improved reporting model; that document's “implemented & verified” claim does not establish that the CEO sees it. The old `reworkV3Experience` flag infrastructure exists, but the active homepage and notice route do not consult it to select the current journey. Do not build a console around that flag as though it switches these pages. Legacy public checkout endpoints remain potential callable surfaces and need compatibility/security review even when their card UI is unmounted.

## Experience review: preserve and replace

Visually observed sign-in is restrained, legible at desktop size, clearly identifies the portal and supplies one obvious action. Preserve that simplicity and account-bound authentication. Its small muted return link needs contrast/focus verification; no completed accessibility audit is claimed.

Behind authentication, the following are source-derived: the global Lob banner and four KPI cards precede campaigns, recovery cards, visitor telemetry, font/occasion cohorts, then orders (`app/admin/page.tsx:1194–1262`). Large repeated rounded containers, `space-y-8`, padding up to 32px, and explanatory marketing copy consume executive attention before obligations. The Orders tab combines at least six jobs; money/campaign data repeats in the margins tab and order drawer. This raises investigation effort; the amount of scrolling and task-time impact needs fixture-based visual validation.

Keep search, status filtering, proof inspection, device-responsive order cards, order drilldown, incident categories, code management, comps, and application flags. Replace font/occasion cohorts with notice, tier, entry surface and service evidence for current DocketStamp orders; retain a labeled legacy archive only for compatible historical records. Valuation remains an assumptions tool under Planning, outside reported business performance.

Tables and controls use many 10–12px labels and muted stone colors; dense pages should use fewer containers and readable type rather than shrink further. Mobile horizontal tab scrolling plus bottom nav duplicates navigation; use one compact primary navigation with More. A phone should show urgent obligations before detailed campaign charts. Avoid repeating private addresses and messages across overview/table/drawer: reveal only in authorized detail. Some buttons use browser alerts/confirms, truncated titles, and icon-only affordances; test keyboard, dialog semantics, focus return, accessible names, and touch size. Source alone cannot establish computed contrast or actual screen-reader behavior.

No evidence reviewed demonstrates these design issues caused lost conversions. The commercial case is preventing incorrect decisions and making actual service failures discoverable and recoverable.
